Schematic

The netlist as a diagram. Thick horizontal lines are the power buses; components tap them with matching-color drops; blue lines are Pico signals. Relays and buttons are drawn once — all four channels are identical.

Read it top-down: 12 V comes in, the buck makes the 5 V bus, the AMS1117 taps 5 V and makes the 3.5 V bus for the coils. The Pico sits on 5 V and fans signals out — GP6–9 to the relays, GP0–3 + GP10–13 to the buttons. Everything returns to the GND bus.

Relay load side

This is the one part that depends on what each device actually is. Use COM + NO so every load is off at boot. These wires exit the lower deck straight to the trailer — they never come up to the electronics deck.

RelayCOMNOState at boot
1 · Water Heatersource→ loadOFF
2 · Water Pumpsource→ loadOFF
3 · Exterior Lightssource→ loadOFF
4 · Interior Lightssource→ loadOFF
Check current before you trust these contacts. The relay is rated 10 A. A 12 V pump or LED lighting is fine. But if your water heater switches a mains (120 V AC) electric element, that can pull 10–12 A and exceeds this relay — drive a proper contactor with the relay instead of switching the element directly. Confirm each load's voltage and current against the 10 A / 250 V AC / 30 V DC rating first.
D

Bring-up order

Set voltages before anything downstream is connected — an unset AMS1117 pot can sit near 4.5 V and cook the 3 V coils.

  1. Bench the MP1584 alone, set 5.00 V, meter it. Then wire the 5 V rail.
  2. Bench the AMS1117 from 5 V, set ~3.5 V, meter it — before it touches any relay VCC.
  3. Buzz out every row above with a meter (continuity + no shorts rail-to-GND). Tick as you go.
  4. Power 12 V. Confirm 5 V and 3.5 V rails present; confirm the Pico boots (RGB status).
  5. Confirm data/config.json has LedPin 10–13, then pio run -e pico2w --target buildunified → flash. Monitor 115200.
  6. Press each button → its relay and LED follow. Toggle from the web UI → the LED tracks too.
  7. Relays should latch crisply off the stiff 3.5 V. If one is lazy, nudge the AMS1117 toward ~3.6 V (still safe for a 3 V coil).
